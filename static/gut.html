The same engine, pointed at the gut functional world: ask a plasmid, resistance-gene, or strain-tracking question and get the public datasets that can answer it — each depth-vetted for whether the sequencing is deep enough to do the work.

Ten open human-gut datasets, found by literature discovery (EuropePMC), cited, and vetted from ENA run-level bases. It mixes landmark cohorts (Li 2016 FMT strain engraftment, Poyet BIO-ML, the Forster HBC isolate collection) with recent studies, and extends MiDAS beyond its mucosal catalog into gut mobile genetic elements, resistance genes, and strain-level analysis.

Prototype, not a finished catalog. Ten datasets, discovered and vetted automatically to show the method works in this domain; a production version would be larger and hand-curated. Depth is computed from ENA run-level bases (WGS-filtered), so a dataset can be shotgun yet only partial. Isolate-genome collections (HBC) are strain-resolved by construction, so they skip the depth bar. Curation matters: a non-human (animal) resistome dataset the discovery returned was excluded by hand.
